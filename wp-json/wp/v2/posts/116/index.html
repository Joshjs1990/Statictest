Groups of protesters gathered outside several hotels on Wednesday night, voicing opposition to what they believe are Immigration and Customs Enforcement (ICE) agents staying at the locations. The demonstrations took place in multiple cities, with participants aiming to draw attention to the presence of federal agents in their communities.<\/p>\n\n

Protests Target Hotel Locations<\/h2>\n\n

According to organizers, the protests were coordinated to coincide with reports that ICE agents were being housed at certain hotels. Demonstrators assembled outside these establishments, chanting and holding signs to express their disapproval of the agents\u2019 presence. Some protesters used noisemakers and megaphones in an effort to make their message heard by those inside the hotels.<\/p>\n\n

Community Concerns Raised<\/h2>\n\n

Organizers stated that the demonstrations were intended to highlight concerns about ICE operations and their impact on local immigrant communities. \u201cWe want to make it clear that ICE is not welcome here,\u201d one protester said during the event. Participants also called on hotel management to reconsider allowing federal agents to stay at their properties.<\/p>\n\n

Hotel Management Response<\/h2>\n\n

Hotel representatives did not immediately respond to requests for comment regarding the presence of ICE agents or the protests outside their buildings. It remains unclear whether the hotels were aware of the agents\u2019 affiliations prior to the demonstrations.<\/p>\n\n

ICE Has Not Issued a Statement<\/h2>\n\n

As of Thursday morning, Immigration and Customs Enforcement had not released a statement addressing the protests or confirming whether agents were staying at the hotels in question. The agency\u2019s activities in the area have been the subject of ongoing public debate.<\/p>\n\n

Further Developments Possible<\/h2>\n\n

Organizers indicated that similar demonstrations may occur in the future if ICE agents continue to be housed at local hotels. Community groups have stated they will continue to monitor the situation and advocate for the interests of immigrant residents.<\/p>\n\n
