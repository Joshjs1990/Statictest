Marriott International has implemented changes to its online reservation system, removing the option for guests to type in custom requests during the booking process. This adjustment comes after reports that the company had previously not responded to many such requests submitted through its platform.<\/p>\n\n

Previously, guests making reservations through Marriott\u2019s website or app could enter specific requests\u2014such as asking for extra pillows, a crib, or a particular room location\u2014by typing them into a designated field. However, this feature has now been replaced with a limited selection of preset options, preventing guests from submitting personalized notes or requirements in advance.<\/p>\n\n

According to Marriott, the decision to eliminate the free-text request box was made to streamline operations and ensure that requests are handled more efficiently. A spokesperson for the company stated, \u201cWe are committed to enhancing the guest experience by focusing on requests that can be fulfilled consistently across our properties.\u201d<\/p>\n\n

Travelers have expressed concerns that the removal of the custom request field could make it more difficult to communicate specific needs, especially for those with accessibility requirements or unique preferences. Some guests noted that even before the change, requests entered online were often not acknowledged or fulfilled by hotel staff.<\/p>\n\n

Marriott has indicated that guests can still communicate special requirements by contacting the hotel directly after booking. The company advises travelers to call the property or use the Marriott Bonvoy app\u2019s chat function to relay any particular needs not covered by the preset options.<\/p>\n\n

The adjustment to Marriott\u2019s booking system follows similar actions by other major hotel chains, which have also limited or removed the ability for guests to submit custom requests online. Industry analysts suggest that these changes may be aimed at reducing operational complexity and ensuring that only requests that can be reliably accommodated are accepted.<\/p>\n\n
