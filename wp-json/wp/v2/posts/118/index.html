Nearly 15,000 nurses initiated a strike on Monday at two of New York City\u2019s largest hospitals, following unsuccessful contract negotiations focused on staffing levels and pay increases.<\/p>\n\n

Strike Commences After Negotiations Stall<\/h2>\n\n

The walkout began early Monday morning at Mount Sinai Hospital in Manhattan and three locations of Montefiore Medical Center in the Bronx. The New York State Nurses Association, representing the striking nurses, stated that the main issues in dispute are inadequate staffing and insufficient wage increases. Negotiations between the union and hospital management continued through the weekend but failed to produce an agreement before the strike deadline.<\/p>\n\n

Hospitals Respond to Staff Shortages<\/h2>\n\n

Both Mount Sinai and Montefiore Medical Center reported that they had implemented contingency plans to maintain patient care during the strike. These measures include diverting ambulances, postponing elective surgeries, and transferring some patients to other facilities. Hospital officials indicated that they had brought in temporary nurses and reassigned administrative staff with clinical backgrounds to assist with patient care.<\/p>\n\n

Union Emphasizes Need for Improved Conditions<\/h2>\n\n

Union leaders have emphasized that the strike centers on ensuring safe staffing ratios and fair compensation. \u201cNurses don\u2019t want to strike. Hospital executives have pushed us to this point,\u201d said Nancy Hagans, president of the New York State Nurses Association, during a press conference on Monday. She added, \u201cWe are fighting to improve patient care and to protect our profession.\u201d<\/p>\n\n

Hospitals Highlight Efforts to Reach Agreement<\/h2>\n\n

Mount Sinai Hospital and Montefiore Medical Center stated that they have offered wage increases and commitments to hire additional nurses. In a statement, Mount Sinai said, \u201cWe are dismayed by the union\u2019s decision to walk away from the bedside of their patients.\u201d Montefiore Medical Center expressed disappointment as well, stating that it had proposed a 19.1% wage increase over three years and new staffing ratios.<\/p>\n\n

Impact on Patients and City Services<\/h2>\n\n

City officials reported that emergency services remain operational, but some delays and disruptions are expected. The New York City Fire Department said that ambulances are being diverted from the affected hospitals to other facilities as needed. Mayor Eric Adams urged both sides to continue negotiations, stating, \u201cOur top priority is patient safety.\u201d<\/p>\n\n

Broader Context of Healthcare Labor Disputes<\/h2>\n\n

This strike is among the largest in the city\u2019s healthcare sector in recent years. It follows a series of labor actions by nurses and other healthcare workers across the United States, as staff shortages and pandemic-related pressures have intensified disputes over working conditions and pay.<\/p>\n\n
